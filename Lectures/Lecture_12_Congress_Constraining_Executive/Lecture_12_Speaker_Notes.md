# Lecture 12 Speaker Notes — Congress Constraining the Executive

**Next:** Limiting Congress

## Empowerment

Article I as institutional check on the executive; tax power as continuation of the long struggle over the purse; war powers that still sat with George III in 1789 assigned to Congress.

## Problem: fused power

King as lawgiver, enforcer, and judge. Parliament has been pushing back for a long time (Five Members).

## The break

Congress makes law; executive enforces; courts judge. Art. I §1 vesting clause.

## Tax path

1. Magna Carta — consent to extraordinary levies
2. Petition of Right 1628 — no forced loans / non-Parliamentary taxes
3. Ship Money — crown tests the limit
4. Bill of Rights 1689 — levying by prerogative without Parliament illegal
5. Article I — tax in Congress; revenue bills in the House; appropriations by law

## War power

Britain 1789: crown still has formal prerogative to declare war; Parliament has the purse. U.S.: Congress declares war; raise armies (2-year appropriation limit); navy; marque and reprisal. Defer the War Powers Resolution to Lecture 17.
