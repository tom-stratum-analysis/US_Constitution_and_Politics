# Lecture 13 Speaker Notes — Limiting Congress

**Next content:** Trade-offs / unintended consequences

## Empowerment

Why majority rule still needs limits; enumerated and prohibited powers; other branches and Bill of Rights as constraints on Congress.

## Why limit Congress

- Not only kings: legislative majority can still tyrannize.
- Aristotle (`gemini_aristotle_2.jfif`): democracy as many ruling for themselves.
- Madison (`madison_democracy.jfif`): majority faction.
- Crisis + misinformation → overreact relative to long-run progress.
- 1789 point (spoken): people mostly solved their own problems; Congress could still break what worked.

## Enumerated / N&P

- `enumerated.jpg` — herein granted; list not blank check.
- Necessary and Proper: original *necessary and proper*, not convenient / "close enough."

## Prohibited / Bill of Rights

- §9 categories: criminal-process (habeas, attainder, ex post facto); anti-aristocracy / anti-corruption (titles of nobility, foreign emoluments).
- Stress the Ninth and Tenth.
- Elections: Congress may regulate federal elections, but primary presumption is state-run.

## Structural brakes

- Bicameralism — two houses must agree.
- Original Senate: state legislatures appointed Senators (17th changed this).
- Presentment / veto.
- Judicial review as hard outer limit; cases later.
- 27th Amendment — pay changes effective only after next House election.

When block a majority? Why narrow N&P? Is judicial review republican or anti-majoritarian?
