# Lecture 5 Speaker Notes — Origins of Government

**Course:** USGOVT101

## Empowerment

Two answers to where government comes from: Olson (empirical) and Locke (normative rights/legitimacy). Both matter for why we limit power later.

## Collective action

Ostrom line is the hook. Prisoner’s Dilemma video is for in-class show. Without enforcement, cooperation collapses; that is a core justification for coercive state capacity — and for worrying about that capacity.

Security as first public good: Who provides? Who pays?

## Olson (empirical)

Roving vs stationary bandit. Stationary bandit’s long-term tax interest → incentive to protect production and provide order.

Speaker only: democracy can be framed as a more inclusive form of stationary banditry. Useful provocation; keep out of the projected text.

One-line takeaway: order can grow out of self-interested violence. Descriptive, not a moral blessing.

## Locke (normative)

State of nature ≠ Hobbesian war. Inconveniences: no settled law, no neutral judge, no reliable enforcement. Rights pre-exist; government protects, does not create.

US founding as a rare written instance of the theoretical idea, not proof that every state was born that way.

## Discussion

- Does Olson make government look more dangerous or more inevitable?
- Does Locke’s contract still bind people who never signed?
