# Speaker Notes — Lecture 21: Court Cases Wrap-up

Speech + civil-rights landmarks. Keep cases as one-idea slides.

## Speech

Schenck (1917) / Holmes "clear and present danger" is the starting point, not the current test. *Brandenburg v. Ohio* (1969) is the teaching rule: directed to inciting imminent lawless action, and likely to produce it. That leaves a lot of vile speech protected. Say so.

*NYT v. United States* if you want a prior-restraint beat. *NYT v. Sullivan* if you want press + public officials. Do not do both unless time is fat.

*Tinker* (1969) is the campus hook heading into Direct Action.

## Civil rights cases

*Plessy* (1896) → *Brown* (1954). Brown is both a constitutional holding and an incomplete social fact. *Loving* (1967) is the clean "majority may not sort people by race in marriage law" line.

Callback to Lecture 7: amendments expanded the circle; cases sometimes enforced the expansion against states that would not.
